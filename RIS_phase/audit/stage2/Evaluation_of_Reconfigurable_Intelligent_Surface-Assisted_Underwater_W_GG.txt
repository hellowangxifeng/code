A. Problem Statement
Signal loss in underwater environment is common due
to the occlusion or blockage by aquatic plants (e.g., hy-
drophytes, macrophytes), underwater vehicles (e.g., sub-
marines), seamounts, and environmental conditions (e.g., tur-
bidity, salinity). Consequently, an UWOC system can face signal
loss, which cause burst errors. In addition to this, random errors
also occurs due to the effect of turbulence and pointing errors.
As a result larger amount of information loss in the UWOC
communication system. A RIS-assisted UWOC system can be
used to bypass occlusion or blockage during communication.
The contributions of our work are as follows:
r A RIS-assisted UWOC channel is characterized by com-
bining underwater turbulence model, beam attenuation,
occlusion or blockage due to obstacles and pointing errors
associated to beam jitter and intelligent channel reconﬁg-
urable node jitter. The probability density function (PDF)
andcumulativedistributionfunction(CDF) expressions for
the N RIS elements based UWOC are derived in terms of
signal-to-noise ratio (SNR).
r Analytical expressions of the BER, OP and CC of the RIS-
assisted UWOC system are derived and plotted according
to the average SNR.
r The generalized BER expressions are derived for various
modulation schemes, namely, binary phase shift keying
(BPSK), binary frequency shift keying (BFSK) with re-
spect to coherent and non-coherent detection schemes.
Similarly, the analytical expressions of CC for the het-
erodyne detection (HD) and intensity modulation direct
detection (IMDD) schemes have been evaluated.
r The numerical BER results are corroborated with the
Monte-Carlo simulations.
The remainder of this paper is organized as follows: The
system and channel models are presented in Section II. Then, the
system performance in terms of BER, OP and CC are analyzed in
Section III. The corresponding numerical results and discussion
are presented in Section IV. Finally, conclusions are drawn in
Section V.
