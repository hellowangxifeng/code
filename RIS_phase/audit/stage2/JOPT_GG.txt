Abstract
In receiver diversity systems, signal replicas will suffer from channel correlations if there are
insufﬁcient spacings for component receivers. Knowledge of the channel correlation is
important for system performance assessment and improvement. In this paper, we give a
theoretical study of the channel correlation in receiver diversity systems. Based on the
plane-wave model and the extended Rytov theory for the ABCD ray-matrix formulation,
expressions for the channel correlation are derived under general atmospheric conditions. The
effects of antenna spacing, turbulence strength, receiver aperture size, and wavelength are
examined in detail and the corresponding physical mechanisms for the results are discussed.
An approximation for estimating the channel correlation length under strong turbulence
conditions is obtained.
Keywords: free-space optical communication, atmospheric turbulence, spatial diversity,
channel correlation

1. Introduction
Free-space
optical
(FSO)
communication
has
become
increasingly
popular
in
recent
years
for
its
potential
high-data-rate capacity, low cost, and particularly wide
bandwidth on unregulated spectra. However, the laser beams
are susceptible to irradiance ﬂuctuations induced by the
atmospheric turbulence. This irradiance ﬂuctuation, known as
optical scintillation [1–3], can give rise to power losses and
error probability. It is the major performance impairment in
FSO systems.
Spatial diversity is an effective technique to mitigate
scintillation [4–6]. By the use of multiple transmitters (trans-
mitter diversity) or multiple receivers (receiver diversity) or
a combination of both, subchannels are formed to transmit
signal replicas. Based on the fact that multiple uncorrelated
subchannels have a lower probability of experiencing deep
fades simultaneously, scintillation can be reduced substan-
tially. By assuming that subchannels are independent of each
other, vast works [7–9] (and references therein) have been
devoted to studying the performance of systems with different
detection, modulation and signal combining schemes.
In practice, to ensure the validity of the independence
assumption in the works above, any two adjacent antennas
(transmitters or receivers) must be sufﬁciently far apart.
Otherwise, channel correlations will arise among subchannels
and degrade the system performance [10–12]. To deter-
mine the spacings for component antennas, some experi-
ments [13–15] undertaking a similar task have been reported,
but due to the hardware limitation, only isolated points in
design space could be evaluated and thus physical insight into
the channel correlation cannot be comprehensively revealed
from the results. In order to conduct design studies that probe
a large volume of the relevant design space, Anguita et al

the Rytov theory [18], it is only valid for point receivers under

as well as the receiver aperture effects. To the best of our
knowledge, a well-established theoretical model including
these factors is still not available for the aperture diversity
technique.
In this paper, we present a theoretical study of channel
correlation in receiver diversity systems under general
turbulence conditions, including the aperture effects and other
link parameters. In section 2, we derive the expressions for
the channel correlation coefﬁcient for a pair of component
receivers based on the plane-wave model and the extended
Rytov theory for ABCD ray-matrix formulation. In section 3,
we illustrate the properties of the channel correlation in detail
including the effects of receiver separation, aperture sizes,
turbulence strength and wavelength. Section 4 is reserved
for our conclusion. The derivation details are presented in
appendix A.

Figure 3 shows typical curves for the channel correlation
coefﬁcient γ12 as a function of separation distance for
different turbulence conditions and aperture sizes. One can
observe that for a ﬁxed turbulence strength σ 2
R and a
ﬁxed aperture diameter D, the channel correlation decreases
monotonously with increasing separation distance d12, and
goes across a zero point where the channel correlation length
ρc is deﬁned, i.e. γ12(ρc) = 0. After passing the zero point,
the curve approaches zero with a long negative tail. This
behavior coincides with the general concept that when the two
channels are overlapped, they must be perfectly correlated.
But when the two channels are separated inﬁnitely far apart,
they must be independent of each other. Note that the negative
tail denotes separation distances in which, on average, one
receiver is in a bright region of the ﬁeld and the other is
in a dark region [18]. In general, two separated receivers
are treated as independent of each other when the separation
distance d12 is larger than ρc, though it may represent negative
correlation. It is also shown that the channel correlation is
affected by turbulence strength and aperture size, which will
be illustrated in ﬁgures 4 and 5, respectively.
Figure 4 illustrates the dependence of the channel
correlation on turbulence strength. Shown is γ12 versus
the refractive-index structure constant C2
n for three different
separation distances. One can observe that for each case,
channel correlation arises only when the channel correlation
length exceeds the separation distance due to the increase of
C2
n. As C2
n continues to increase, γ12 increases accordingly.
For a small separation distance, γ12 can even reach a very
high level (as in the case of d12 = 15 cm) that can degrade the
system performance substantially. Channel correlation can be
reduced effectively by using a larger separation distance, as
shown in the cases of d12 = 20 and 25 cm.

4. Conclusion
In this paper we give a theoretical study of the channel
correlation in receiver diversity systems for FSO commu-
nication. Based on the plane-wave model, expressions for
the correlation coefﬁcient are derived by using the extended
Rytov theory for ABCD ray-matrix formulation. By using
these expressions, characteristics of the channel correlation
are examined in detail. An estimate for the channel correlation
length under strong turbulence conditions is obtained. From
the applications point of view, our formulation provides a
theoretical way for evaluating the channel correlation under
general link conditions, which is important for the prediction
of system performance.
