In addressing the limitations of NLOS-UWOC setups, re-
searchers have delved into two distinct types of OIRS: mirror-
array (MA)-based and meta-surface-based [6]. MA-based OIRS
function akin to controllable mirrors, meticulously directing
reﬂected beams towards the speciﬁc users [7]. On the other
hand, meta-surface-based OIRS utilize sub-lambda-sized atoms
to delay or phase-shift incoming signals, facilitating constructive
interference at the destination.
In the context of free-space optical (FSO) communication
systems, a novel approach involving phased array (PA) and MA
technologies for FSO-based OIRS has been proposed in [8].
This method offers advantages such as cost-effectiveness and
extended reach, making it suitable for various communication
system applications. Similar to RF-based intelligent reﬂecting
surface (RF-IRS), a PA-based OIRS comprises programmable
phased modulation units. Both MA and PA-based OIRS allow
for beam convergence, deﬂection, and splitting, enhancing the
versatility and efﬁciency of wireless optical communication
systems [8]. Further, in [9], a signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) based
mirror assignment (SMA) scheme optimizes mirror element al-
location in OIRS for enhanced user throughput and fairness, out-
performing conventional methods. The SMA approach promises
fair transmission rates in multi-user scenarios. In the domain of
indoor visible light communication (VLC), the utilization of
OIRS has been explored to enhance performance. For instance,
in [10], an OIRS was employed in an indoor VLC system, with
considerations for receiver orientation. The system’s perfor-
mance was evaluated in terms of achievable data rate and outage.
Furthermore, the potential of OIRS in multi-user multiple input
multiple output (MIMO) systems has been investigated in [11].
Here, OIRS facilitates data transmission for both the uplink and
downlink phases, enhancing system efﬁciency. In [12], the au-
thors optimize MIMO VLC systems with OIRS, enhancing ca-
pacity by aligning OIRS elements and deriving optimal emission
power. Two proposed algorithms, location-aided interior-point
(LIP) algorithm and the log-det-based alternating optimization
(LDAO) algorithm, show signiﬁcant capacity improvements
and superior performance in numerical results. Physical layer
security has also been enhanced through the use of OIRS, as
explored in [13]. The authors investigated how OIRS can create
a ﬂexible wireless environment to increase secrecy rates.
A. Related Works
1) Mixed Terristrial-UWC System: In the study detailed
in [14], the investigation focuses on the outage probability,
average bit error rate (BER), and ergodic capacity. The research
implements amplify and forward (AF) and decode and forward
(DF) relays across various water types in the terrestrial link
of a mixed RF-UWOC system, incorporating a RF-IRS. Liang
Yang et al. in [15] consider a mixed FSO-UWOC dual-hop
transmission system, incorporating heterodyne detection and in-
tensity modulation with direct detection (IM/DD). They develop
closed-form expressions for outage probability, average BER,
and average capacity, assuming gamma gamma (GG) fading for
the FSO channel and exponential and generalized gamma (EGG)
fading with pointing errors for the UWOC channel. In [16], the
authors explore the performance of a triple-hop IRS-assisted
RF-FSO system convergent with UWOC. They investigate the
average BER and outage probability, considering Nakagami-m
fading over the IRS-RF link and GG fading over the IRS-FSO
and UWOC links. Our work [17] examines the performance of
IRS-assisted and DF relay-assisted links within an integrated
RF-UWOC system. Key ﬁndings underscore the inﬂuence of
buoy positioning relative to the underwater destination, with
the UWOC link exhibiting the highest spectral efﬁciency (SE)
but limited range. The RF link parameters are critical within a
speciﬁc distance range. Moreover, the study demonstrates that
the IRS link outperforms the DF relay link and the direct link
in SE under speciﬁc conditions, albeit with reduced energy
efﬁciency (EE). Importantly, IRS proves valuable when the
direct path is obstructed, suggesting its potential for enhancing
system performance in challenging underwater environments.
2) Completely UWC System: Huang et al. demonstrated the
use of mirrors for underwater signal reﬂection in their study [18],
extending light transmission distance within a water tank setup.
The LOS and NLOS models in underwater sensor networks
were explored by authors in [19], utilizing the water-air in-
terface as a mirror for NLOS communication. Tang et al.
investigated NLOS UWOC path loss through Monte Carlo
simulations in [20], emphasizing the signiﬁcance of consid-
ering sea surface slopes and seawater scattering properties.
Yildiz et al. introduced a closed-form expression for under-
water path loss in [21], emphasizing optimal NLOS reﬂection
gains. Meanwhile, authors highlighted the potential of IRS in
various challenging environments in [22], with emphasis on
underground and underwater Internet of Things (IoT) scenarios.
Kisseleff et al. examined the beneﬁts of IRS deployment in
challenging environments in [23], showcasing its reliability in
mitigating challenges like attenuation and turbulence-induced
effects. In [24], Naik et al. evaluated the performance of a
meta-surface based OIRS considering various factors such as
underwater turbulence and beam direction errors. Similarly, Ata
et al. utilized the oceanic turbulence optical power spectrum
(OTOPS) model to analyze the characteristics of the underwater
turbulent environment and highlighted the reliability enhance-
ments that IRS bring to UWOC systems in [25]. Our prior
investigation in [26] explores the potential of OIRS in enhancing
UWOC systems, evaluating key metrics such as SE and out-
age probability. Furthermore, in [27], we investigated dynamic
strategies for optimizing UWOC systems, aiming to enhance
system performance and adaptability in challenging underwater
environments. Through adaptive mirror assignment, the study
seeks to improve efﬁciency and effectiveness in UWOC systems.
Recently, in [28], the authors investigated the application of si-
multaneous transmit and reﬂect (STAR)-IRS in UWOC systems,
demonstrating its effectiveness in addressing the underwater
challenges.
