Abstract—Intelligent reflecting surfaces (IRS) are emerging
as a promising technology for establishing adaptable links
and addressing the limitations of direct line-of-sight (LOS) in
underwater optical wireless communication (UOWC) systems.
In challenging underwater environments where direct LOS
is often disrupted, the IRS can provide alternative paths
for stable and reliable communication. In this paper, we
consider an IRS-assisted UOWC system with an autonomous
surface vehicle (ASV) and autonomous underwater vehicles
(AUVs) operating in realistic, dynamic conditions. To enable
adaptive link switching between LOS and IRS-based paths,
we apply a reinforcement learning approach using a deep Q-
network (DQN). We conduct simulations over gamma-gamma
channels, taking into account key degrading factors, including
underwater attenuation, pointing errors, turbulence, and ob-
struction. System performance is evaluated in terms of both
average reward and cumulative reward. Based on the obtained
results, we demonstrate that the proposed method effectively
maintains transmission reliability in the presence of obstacles,
improving performance across various scenarios, particularly
under obstruction, low attenuation, and minimal pointing error
conditions.
Index Terms—underwater optical wireless communication
(UOWC), intelligent reflecting surfaces (IRS), reinforcement
learning (RL), deep Q-network.
I. INTRODUCTION
Optical wireless communication (OWC) technologies
have emerged as practical alternatives for wireless commu-
nication across various hybrid applications, including terres-
trial outdoor links [1], indoor settings [2], and underwater
links for onshore and offshore scenarios [3], [4]. Despite
their advantages in terms of higher data rates and broader
bandwidth compared to traditional radio-frequency (RF) and
acoustic link communication, underwater OWC (UOWC)
systems face challenges due to the complex characteristics of
underwater channels and the presence of natural obstructions
[5], [6]. Maintaining alignment is critical, as even brief
obstructions can cause burst errors and significant data loss
due to high transmission speeds.
To address these challenges, flexible and reconfigurable
links are essential for supporting longer-range, reliable
UOWC systems. Intelligent reflecting surfaces (IRS) offer a
promising solution by providing alternative communication
paths, thereby reducing the stringent need for line of sight
(LOS) alignment between the transmitter (Tx) and receiver
(Rx) [7], [8]. An IRS-assisted free-space optical (FSO)
communication link has been proposed to alleviate such
requirement for a direct LOS [9]. Inspired by the advantages
of IRS in FSO links, this concept has also been considered
for UOWC links [10]. However, the dynamic underwater
environment poses challenges for communications using
IRS. While several deterministic optimization techniques
have been applied in underwater contexts, their effectiveness
diminishes in such variable conditions. Therefore, a dynamic
approach that does not rely on comprehensive knowledge of
the environment is needed. Machine learning, particularly
reinforcement learning (RL), presents a promising solution
for efficiently addressing these challenges [11].
Recognizing the potential use of IRS in UOWC sys-
tems, recent research has focused on their implementation
[10]–[13]. For instance, [10] introduced an IRS-assisted
UOWC system designed to mitigate the impacts of obsta-
cles, pointing errors, and turbulence. The authors in [12]
quantified the performance improvements achieved through
IRS technology considering an underwater turbulence optical
power spectrum model. In [11], the authors proposed beam
adaptation strategies based on RL, including Q-learning and
SARSA, to address pointing-acquisition-and-tracking (PAT)
challenges. [13] explored a machine-learning approach for
power-efficient communication by adjusting the beam di-
vergence angle and transmission power based on varying
underwater conditions. Despite these advancements, none
of the studies have considered employing IRS for link-
switching in UOWC systems in conjunction with machine
learning.
In this paper, we propose a link-switching method that
utilizes IRS for UOWC systems in realistic scenarios with
obstructions. Our approach considers two types of links: a
direct link and an IRS-based link. By employing an RL
approach, we enable dynamic switching between these links
based on underwater conditions, facilitating seamless com-
