Abstract—Underwater
wireless
optical
communication
(UWOC) enables high-speed, low-latency links for the Internet
of Underwater Things (IoUT), but remains highly vulnerable to
complex and dynamic channel impairments. In practice, slant
transmission paths frequently occur due to spatial oﬀsets between
underwater sensors and marine surface vehicles (MSVs), where
the link quality is jointly aﬀected by MSV-induced misalignment,
depth-dependent attenuation, and oceanic turbulence. Most
existing models address these factors independently or under
static assumptions, limiting their applicability. To address this
challenge, this article proposes a novel modeling and simulation
framework that jointly captures the coupled eﬀects of dynamic
posture,
stratiﬁed
absorption
and
scattering,
and
spatially
varying turbulence along slant UWOC paths. A parallelized
wave optics simulation (WOS) platform is developed using
Argo ﬂoat data to reﬂect realistic ocean conditions. Leveraging
this dataset, a convolutional neural network-based fast channel
simulation method (CNN-FCSM) is designed, achieving over
98% prediction accuracy of received optical intensity, while
reducing runtime by more than 90% compared with WOS.
Comprehensive
evaluations
demonstrate
that
the
proposed
approach enables accurate and scalable estimation of average
bit error rate (ABER) and outage probability (OP) without
relying on predeﬁned statistical fading models, oﬀering a robust
tool for dynamic UWOC analysis and IoUT system design in
complex underwater environments.
Index Terms—Channel characterization, convolutional neural
network (CNN), performance prediction, slant underwater opti-
cal communication.

Internet of Underwater Things (IoUT), sensing data volumes
are rapidly increasing, exceeding the capacity of traditional
acoustic links due to limited bandwidth and high latency [1].
To overcome these constraints, underwater wireless optical
communication (UWOC) has emerged as a promising solution,
oﬀering high data rate, low latency, and secure transmission
[2]. UWOC is particularly critical for real-time data oﬄoading
from deep-sea nodes to surface gateways, though establishing
reliable long-range links remains a major challenge.
UWOC channels are highly vulnerable to dynamic and slant
transmission conditions that undermine link reliability. Plat-
form motion and water currents cause frequent misalignment,
while slant paths extend propagation distance and intensify
fading and attenuation [3]. These eﬀects collectively degrade
signal quality, underscoring the need for channel models that
explicitly account for dynamic and slant conditions in IoUT
deployment.
A. Related Works
Most prior works on UWOC channel modeling have
focused on ﬁxed transmission conﬁgurations, particularly
horizontal and vertical links. Horizontal conﬁgurations are
typical for near-ground sensor networks, where the trans-
mitter and receiver are positioned at the same depth. The
absence of vertical variability allows for simpliﬁed mod-
eling of turbulence-induced fading, such as log-normal,
gamma–gamma, K, and exponentiated Weibull distribution
[4], [5], [6], [7]. Vertical UWOC modeling has also received
attention, particularly in the context of depth-dependent atten-
uation and turbulence. Several studies have investigated light
absorption and scattering using depth-varying chlorophyll-
based models [8], [9], leading to analytical impulse response
ﬁtting using various parametric distributions, such as double
gamma [10], beta prime [11], a combination of exponential
and arbitrary power function [12]. Many studies approxi-
mate the vertical turbulence link as a series of horizontally
uniform layers and apply identical fading statistics across
them [13], [14], [15], [16], [17]. However, this approach
overlooks the cumulative and correlated nature of wavefront
distortion through stratiﬁed turbulence. Despite these eﬀorts,
the prior work has largely overlooked slant UWOC geometries,
which are prevalent in practical deployments involving marine
surface vehicless (MSVs) and deep-sea nodes. Such links are

V. CONCLUSION
In this article, a dynamic slant UWOC channel model that
comprehensively accounts for depth-dependent absorption,
scattering, slant oceanic turbulence, and the posture-induced
misalignment is developed. To facilitate accurate light propa-
gation modeling and reduce computational cost, we optimized
the implementation of WOS with a parallelized algorithm.
Given the inherent mathematical complexity of the WOS
approach, we proposed a DL-based CNN-FCSM. It is impor-
tant to emphasize that the proposed CNN-FCSM is not
designed for channel optimization, but rather for computa-
tional acceleration of multiparameter physical simulations. The
CNN-FCSM serves as a physics-informed surrogate model that
rapidly predicts received optical intensity across diverse envi-
ronmental and geometric conﬁgurations without the need for
iterative optical simulation. Unlike purely data-driven closed
box models, the proposed CNN-FCSM is trained on datasets
generated by WOS with input parameters taken from Argo
ﬂoat measurements. This ensures that the model captures both
theoretical rigor and measurement-informed realism. The pro-
posed CNN-FCSM reduces computational cost by over 90%
compared with WOS while maintaining above 98% prediction
accuracy, making it an eﬃcient tool for UWOC performance
evaluation. Its predictions closely match WOS-derived OP
and ABER results, conﬁrming its reliability and suitability
for real-time, power-constrained IoUT applications. While
the present study focuses on simulation-based validation, we
fully recognize the importance of experimental veriﬁcation for
practical deployment. To this end, we are currently developing
an experimental test platform for dynamic slant-path UWOC.
In future work, we will use the measured data to further
validate and reﬁne the CNN-FCSM predictions, extending
the framework toward a comprehensive simulation-experiment
hybrid model for UWOC system evaluation.
