4. Beam pointing method for maximizing average SNR
In a dynamic network such as high mobility scenario, channel
conditions can change abruptly in short period of time, and obtaining
an optimized beam coordinates in a highly dynamic network may be
impractical, since the optimization problem needs to be re-solved at
each time slot 𝑡to effectively adapt to network dynamics. Furthermore,
the frequent training period for channel estimation is required in a high
dynamic network, and the feedback of CSI to a transmitter introduces
a high signaling overhead. Therefore, it is important to obtain a robust
beam pointing with respect to the dynamics of FSOC channel, i.e., at-
mospheric turbulence and pointing error, for providing a stable link
performance.
In this section, we aim to determine the optimal transmit beam
coordinates that maximize the average received SNR without an in-
stantaneous CSI. We first investigate the statistical expectation of the
received SNR in terms of atmospheric turbulence and pointing error
and derive the optimal transmit beam coordinates to maximize the
average received SNR with Algorithm 1.

Remark 1. The algorithm proposed in Section 3 for obtaining beam
pointing to maximize instantaneous SNR requires instantaneous CSI,
such as atmospheric turbulence fading and jitter between a transmitter
and receiver lens, which incurs high complexity and high signaling
overhead. On the other hand, optimizing average SNR only requires
statistical CSI such as Rytov variance and the standard deviation of the
jitter, and thus it can further reduce system complexity and overhead
which makes it suitable for practical implementation. Besides, under
certain atmospheric conditions, it can be confirmed in Section 5 that
aligning the transmitter’s beam to the centroid of receiver lenses is a
reasonable method to achieve comparable performance to the proposed
algorithm, without requiring any information about channel conditions.

5. Simulation results
In this section, we evaluate the effectiveness of proposed algorithm
through extensive simulations. Although, the proposed algorithm can
be applied to arbitrary receiver arrangement, we consider 7 receiver
lenses arranged in a circular shape with a radius of 0.12 [m] and sepa-
rated by an angle of 𝜋∕5 [rad], depicted in Fig. 3(b) for our simulations.
Note that the distance between receiver lenses should be greater than
𝜌0(𝑧) which guarantees uncorrelated channel from a transmitter lens to
each receiver lens. The detailed simulation parameters are summarized
in Table 1, unless otherwise specified.
5.1. Beam pointing using instantaneous CSI
The performance of the proposed algorithm is compared with the
following benchmarks:
• Pair: Each transmitter aligns its beam to the center of its paired
receiver lens [16–19].
• Centroid: Each transmitter aligns its beam to the centroid of the
receiver lenses [15].
• Maximum pair: Each transmitter directly transmits its beam
towards the center of the receiver lens with the highest channel
gain.
We first examine a SIMO FSOC scenario to demonstrate the effec-
tiveness of Algorithm 1 which maximizes the received SNR using the
instantaneous CSI. In this scenario, the Rytov variance 𝜎2
𝑅and the
standard deviation of the jitter 𝜎𝑝are set to 0.6 and 0.035, respectively.

receiver configuration shown in Fig. 3(b). As seen in this figure, the
SNR function is a convex because the convexity condition in Lemma 1
is satisfied. Thus, the proposed algorithm well finds the global solution
marked by red star for maximizing the received SNR.
